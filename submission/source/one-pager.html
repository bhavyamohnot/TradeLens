Nubra · Product Management Intern case · Problem 03, post-trade review

TradeLens: the monthly review that names the habits costing you money

Bhavya Mohnot · October 2026

My take on the problem

Traders don't skip reviews out of laziness; reviewing is hard. A tradebook is hundreds of buy/sell rows, and a P&L report says how much you lost, not what to change. The losses that matter are repeated behaviours: the instrument that never works for you, the expiry-day option punt, the trade taken ten minutes after a loss. Each is invisible one trade at a time and obvious across a month.

87.7%of individual F&O traders lost money in FY26
₹91,685 crlost by individual traders in total
~90%of those who lost two years running, and kept trading, lost again

People keep trading; they don't keep learning.

So the job is not "more analytics". It is to take a month of raw order history, name the two or three habits that cost the most rupees, prove each with the actual trades, and hand back one rule to try next month.

Why Nubra should solve it

  • It already has the data. Nothing to export or upload; the review can arrive on the 1st of every month.
  • The right users. Nubra is built for active traders, scalpers and options strategists, which is where habits compound fastest.
  • It can close the loop. A journal app can only tell you about a habit. Nubra can turn it into a guardrail at the moment of decision: an expiry-day nudge on the order ticket, a cool-off after three losses, or pausing an instrument on a smart watchlist.
  • Retention. Traders who blow up their accounts churn. Helping them lose less is good for them and for lifetime value.

The solution space

OptionWhy not (or why)
P&L dashboard (Console-style)Answers "how much", not "what to change"; most traders never open it
Trade journal (TradeZella, Tradervue)Needs manual tagging; too much effort for most retail traders
AI chat over your tradesFlexible but hard to verify; a confident wrong rupee figure does real damage
Opinionated monthly review (chosen)Deterministic checks, ranked by ₹, at most three, each with evidence and a rule
  1. Rank by marginal ₹. If BANKNIFTY losses and closing-hour losses are the same trades, they count once, and the closing hour is cleared.
  2. Don't invent problems. A habit must cover at least 5% of the month's losses and be statistically worse than the trader's other trades. Everything checked and cleared is shown, for example "your quick re-entries made +₹650".
  3. Plain words, no motive-guessing. It says "jumping back in within 15 minutes of a loss", never "revenge trading".

In scope (v1)

  • One month of NSE equity/F&O order history: a broker tradebook CSV/XLSX, or a closed-trade sheet.
  • Round-trip rebuild from fills (scale-ins, partial exits, shorts), with charges estimated when the file has none.
  • Nine habit checks: an instrument that keeps losing · trading on after three losses · quick re-entry after a loss · overtrading · a losing time of day · expiry-day options · holding losers longer than winners · sizing up after a loss · charges eating small trades.
  • A one-rule commitment, checked against next month's review.
  • Runs entirely in the browser; the file never leaves the device.

Out of scope

  • Live positions and margin (problem 02).
  • Broker API sync.
  • Grouping multi-leg strategies (each leg is reviewed on its own).
  • Intraday price paths, so no "you exited too early" claims.
  • Tax P&L, guessing emotions, free-form AI chat.

How I'd know it works

  • Adoption: the share of monthly active traders who open the review, and the share who commit to a rule.
  • Outcome (north star): the ₹ cost of the committed habit the following month against its baseline. Target: at least a 20% drop for half of committers.
  • Trust: the "this is wrong" rate per habit card; a high rate means re-tuning or removing that check.
